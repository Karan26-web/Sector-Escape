# Game Design Document — Sector Escape: The Lost Jungle
*Skill: Area of a Sector of a Circle · A = (θ/360) × πr² · π = 22/7*

> Written for the LXD + production team. The gameplay follows the LXD brief and the 12-panel wireframe, revised by the LXD on 2026-10-23 (arrive → the guardians at the barrier → Karan at his observation point → the sector drawn → enter area → FIRE → three outcomes → exit); this document explains how each beat is implemented, why the maths is wired the way it is, and which knobs exist for tuning.

## LXD review 2026-10-27: choose the values, watch the working, then fire
The number pad is gone from arenas 1–4: the learner chooses, the game works the chosen formula out on screen (right or wrong), and what it gives is fired. Every area in the game is now in terms of π (no 22/7, no decimal anywhere: a decimal radius becomes a fraction in the working, 10.5 = 21/2). The formula A = θ/360 × π × r² heads the dock in every arena.

| Arena | The diagram (top view) | The question | Choices (✓ = the diagram's values) | Working → area |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** r 7, 90° | θ = 90°, r = 7 m (as before) | *Which one gives the area of the sector?* Three formulas, a tap answers | ✓ (90/360) × π × (7)² · (90/180) × π × (7)² · (90/360)² × π × 7 | → 1/4 × π × 49 → **49π/4 m²** (the others: 49π/2 too big, 7π/16 too small) |
| **2** r 10.5, 120° | just **120°** (inside the angle) and **10.5 m**: no θ =, r = | *Complete the formula:* A = (▾/360) × π × (▾)², then FIRE | angle 60° · ✓120° · 30°; radius 21 m · 120 m · ✓10.5 m | → (21/2)² → 1/3 × π × 441/4 → **147π/4 m²** |
| **3** r 14, 90° | no angle number: the **right-angle square** at O; radius **14 m** | A = ▾ × π × (▾)² | ✓1/4 · 1/2 · 1/5; 21 m · 7 m · ✓14 m | → 1/4 × π × 196 → **49π m²** |
| **4** r 14, 135° | **135°** at the angle; the radius **14 m** (the LXD asked for a diameter, 28 m; the user had the radius shown instead, 2026-10-27) | as arena 3 | 3/4 · ✓3/8 · 1/4; 28 m · ✓14 m · 7 m (ours: the LXD said "as in round 3") | → 3/8 × π × 196 → **147π/2 m²** |
| **5–7** | θ = …, r = … (as before) | typed, in terms of π: the π fixed beside the field | — | **80π** (r 20, 72°) · **96π** (r 24, 60°) · **147π** (r 21, 120°): whole numbers |

- **The working** (`ui.formula.solveExpr`, `levels.solveSteps`): the chosen formula appears as written, then a decimal radius becomes a fraction, the fraction reduces and the power is worked out, then A = kπ m² stands in amber; the camera then pulls back and the shot fires that area. Exact fractions throughout.
- **After a wrong shot** the wrong choices are struck out (a half-right drop-down answer keeps its right part, the wrong part turns red and empty), and one tip says what was misread, in the diagram's terms: "A full turn is 360°", "Only the radius is squared", "The angle at O is 120°", "120 is the angle, not the radius", "The square mark at O means 90°: 90/360 = 1/4", "28 m is the diameter: the radius is half of it, 14 m". With choices struck out, arenas 1–4 can never end in a game over (at most two misses); arenas 5–7 keep the typed scaffolding (substitute → cancel → simplify on the 1st miss, the answer on the 2nd).
- **Radii:** arenas 1–2 are back to the LXD's 7 and 10.5 (the earlier π radii 6 and 9 are gone with the typing); arenas 5–6 changed so their answers are whole numbers of π: r 17.5 → **20** (80π), r 21 → **24** (96π), which also makes Thunder Well truly "the largest ring". `validateChain()` checks every chosen arena has its diagram-true choice, and every typed one a whole number of π.
- **Open question for the LXD:** in arena 2, **30° with 21 m gives exactly the right area** (30 × 21² = 120 × 10.5²), so that wrong pair fires a perfect shot and clears the arena. It plays as specified (the area decides the shot); replacing the 30° distractor (e.g. with 90° or 240°) removes it.

## The opening and the controller (2026-10-27): the player walks Karan
After Play on a new game, three scenes set up the journey with the interaction suspended; then the player gets a controller and walks Karan everywhere the game used to walk him.

| Scene (as written) | What the player sees | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Karan is stranded.** Narration: *"Karan has lost his way deep inside an ancient jungle island."* He looks around: water on every side. Karan: *"There's no way back… I need to find another way out."* | A new islet at the causeway's west end (the lake opens into a wide pool round it, so there is water on every side). High over the water the camera closes in on the islet; then it circles Karan, low, while he turns his head; then he faces us with the water behind him for his line | `opening.js`, `chain.js` (`buildIslet`, `lakeOutline`) |
| **2 Karan notices the temple.** The camera moves toward the distant temple; a bright beam of light rises from it. *"Wait… what's that light?"* The camera zooms slightly. *"There's a temple ahead. If there's a way off this island, it must be there."* | Over his shoulder along the causeway toward the temple; a beam of warm light climbs out of the temple's peak into the sky (with a flare and a rising chord), then the camera closes in on the temple. The beam stays for the whole journey and marks the goal from everywhere (not on the title) | `chain.js` (`riseBeam`, `templePeak`) |
| **3 The path is blocked.** He moves toward the temple and reaches the first arena. Narration: *"But the only path to the temple passes through a series of ancient arenas."* *"Looks like I'll have to cross these arenas to reach the temple."* | He runs from the islet onto the causeway (chase camera) and stops 22 m from ring 1's gate; the camera rises over the chain of rings, which leads the eye to the temple and its beam; then it comes down behind him: exactly the pose the walk camera starts from, so the hand-over has no cut | `opening.js` |

- **Presentation:** letterbox bars; the narrator's lines fade in whole over the lower bar; Karan's are typed out in a dialogue box with his face (the user's portrait sheet `assets/ui/karan_portrait.webp`: worried · surprised · determined, one per line; if it ever fails to load, his idle frame cropped to the head stands in) and a soft syllable blip. Each line holds for its reading time; a tap / Space / Enter shows it whole, a second moves on; **Skip ›** cuts straight to the hand-over. Game time, so a pause holds a line. Every game starts from the very start (user 2026-10-27: the title's Continue button is gone): Play always plays the opening (Skip › for those who know it) and goes from it straight into arena 1, and the walkthrough below comes with it. The map takes over after a run (quit, game over, the temple). It replaces the old story card.
- **Karan's run reads the way he moves:** away from the camera → the back sheet's run; across the screen → the front sheet's side run (mirrored to the left); toward the camera → the front run, row `runFront` of `hero_front` (the user's 8-frame strip, added with `tools/add_sheet_row.py … --keep-y --center body`: its hop and bob kept as drawn, each frame centred on head and shoulders so he never sways sideways; source archived outside the project). ~45° either side of straight toward / away, with a little hysteresis so diagonals never flicker (`hero.js`, `_view`). Left or right (the mirroring) is judged on screen.
- **The walk camera stands off his right shoulder** (user 2026-10-27: "when the character is running, it is running sideways"): his whole back sheet (idle, run, cast) is drawn three-quarter, turned up and to the right, so from straight behind his run along the causeway read as running sideways. The camera now follows from 28° round to his right (18° on a portrait phone, whose narrow frame must still hold the goal; `roam.js`, `SWING`), so the path runs up and to the right on screen, the way the art runs. The opening's chase and the run out through the temple gate use the same side. A straight-from-behind run strip would let the camera go back behind him; not needed now.
- **The top view's labels** (user 2026-10-27): no "O" label, the lit point is the centre. θ sits on top of the angle, on its bisector just past the arc, only as far out as it needs to clear both radii (worked out on screen from the label's size, `sector.angleLabelPos`); while the radius sweeps, it circles O at that distance, counting up. The radius label sits beside its line, outside the sector. Checked on every arena on desktop, tablet and phone: nothing off-screen, under the dock or HUD, or overlapping, and the drop-down chips grow round a chosen fraction instead of cutting it.
- **Karan stays solid in the close-ups** (user 2026-10-27, on the "There's no way back…" shot: "the background layer is coming forward"): the older rows of his sheets had a wide half-transparent edge, so the water and the far bank showed through his hair, with a seam at the water line. `tools/harden_alpha.py` tightened the edges of `hero_front` rows 0–4 and of `hero_back` idle and run (the cast row's glow is soft on purpose and was left alone). His sprites also write depth now, so no see-through layer behind him (the lake mist, the barrier glass) can paint over him. The original sheets are archived outside the project.
- **The controller:** a stone stick in the bottom-left corner (touch or mouse; a press anywhere in that corner brings the stick under the thumb) and W A S D / the arrow keys. The walk camera keeps a fixed heading toward the goal and the stick follows that heading (up = ahead), so "up" always means "toward where I need to go" (on screen: up and a little to the right, as the camera stands off his right shoulder). It appears only while Karan is the player's to walk.
- **The walkthrough (with every start from the very beginning):** the stick pops up with a ripple and its knob shows the move until the player takes it; a chime once Karan has walked a few steps (`save.moveTutorialSeen`). No text box beside it (user 2026-10-27: the game is played on tablets, where a W A S D box means nothing; the keys still work on a computer).
- **Restricted to the defined area:** every walk has its own walkable floor (`roam.js`, signed-distance shapes). Karan slides along its edge and can never leave it. Most edges are seen: the parapets and the water (islet + causeway), the ring walls, the barrier across the gap (it ripples where he presses on it). An open passage he may not take shows a short shimmer and a hum where he pushes. The columns are walked round. The causeway ends in a rounded funnel at the door, so a player hugging a parapet still slides into the doorway.
- **Where to go: "highlight the door".** The goal of each walk lights up as Karan comes near it, and fully (with a soft chime, once per pause) whenever the player stands still for 2.4 s or keeps pushing into an edge (`markers.js`):

| Walk | Walkable floor | Goal and its highlight |
|---|---|---|
| The causeway (arena 1) | the islet + the causeway (then the doorway and ring 1) | ring 1's gate: the door glows amber, a glow hangs in the doorway, motes rise. It opens as he reaches it; once he is inside, it slams shut behind him (as before) |
| To the observation spot (every arena) | the ring's floor (the barrier holds the gap) | the spot behind O: a ring of light on the floor with a soft shaft above it (always faintly there: nothing else marks it), brighter as he nears it. On the spot the action begins and he stays there for all of it (LXD) |
| Out of a cleared ring | the ring, its bay, the next ring (the way back stays closed) | the cleared gap: a low curtain of warm light along the arc (an open door) and the amber path through it. The next ring's guardians appear once he is inside |
| The temple court | the court | the temple gate's glow grows; there the gate flares and he runs out through it (the ending, as before) |

- **The walk camera:** behind him at a fixed heading toward the goal (low on the causeway, higher in the big rings, farther and wider on portrait phones). Walls between it and Karan, and walls right in front of it, fade (the bays' walls included now), so the floor he walks on is always in view.
- **Kept automatic:** the action itself (the last step onto the spot, getting back to it after a hit, the knock-down), the cutscenes (gate slam, the reveal, the reward shot, the escape run).
- **Tests:** `?auto=1` / `__se.walk(true)` walk each walk by itself (straight for the goal, sliding along edges); the playtests use it. `?skip=1` still starts inside the ring.
- **Sounds:** Karan's voice blips, the narrator's swell, the beam rising, the stick appearing, the walkthrough's tick, the goal's chime, the edge hum.

## Review 2026-10-27 (Senior GameDev + design): π answers, an overwhelming breach, a volley that bounces
Three problems, each fixed in the build:

| Problem | What the game does now | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Answers in terms of π for arenas 1–3, no decimals anywhere** *(superseded the same day by the LXD review above: arenas 1–4 are chosen, 5–7 typed in terms of π)* | Arenas 1–3 are answered in terms of π. The π is printed, fixed, in cyan beside the answer field (placeholder `?π m²`, the typed number hugs it), so the learner enters only the number in front of π. The old radii could not give whole numbers (r 7 → 12.25π, r 10.5 → 36.75π), so arenas 1–2 got new radii: **r 6, 90° → 9π** · **r 9, 120° → 27π** · r 14, 90° → **49π** (unchanged). Every r, θ and answer is a whole number. r 8 was ruled out: at 90° the circumference 2πr = 16π would equal the answer, so a classic mistake would score. The worked formula keeps π as π (θ/360 crossed out to 1/4 → r × r → r², and on the 2nd miss A = 1/4 × 36π = 9π); the "π = 22/7" note appears only from arena 4. Arena 4 keeps arena 3's radius (14), so the step to 22/7 is a known circle (49π = 154 m²). A new misconception, *π multiplied in* (9 × 22/7 ≈ 28.3 typed in front of the printed π), gets its own tip. `validateChain()` now also checks that π arenas give whole numbers | `levels.js` (`piForm`, `answerFor`, `diagnosePi`), `ui.js` (dock, formula), `index.html`, `main.css` |
| **2 Too small must overwhelm Karan; the guardians stood about** | They stood idle for ~4 s after the glass broke (through the slow-motion crack, the bolts fired one by one, and two pauses), then idled again around Karan. Now the guardians hammer at the glass while it cracks; the instant it gives way the ones the shot missed storm in, and **as many again pour in right behind them** through the same uncovered stretch of the gap (at least 3, up to 16 around Karan). They cut in through the gap, run for places around him (the first in front, the rest wrapping round his sides and back), leap on and keep hammering: blows from every side (a thud, sparks, a jolt each), Karan down in the middle of them and kept in sight. One shield is lost at the first contact. O's pulse throws them off once the whole crowd is on him; he gets up. The uncovered part of the arc is literally where the horde comes from | `bots.js` (`swarm`, `leap`, `maul`, `rally`), `arenaScene.js`, `hero.js` (`overwhelm`, `recover`) |
| **3 Too big: the rays fire at once, and the same ray reflects back** | The shot is a **volley in all three outcomes**: every ray leaves O at the same instant across exactly the wedge the answer asks for (one ray at each guardian it covers, more across the rest, about every 6–10°), and the wedge lights up behind their fronts. The arc's rays strike the glass; an overcharge's rays (red) strike the wall beside the gap, the wall flares and pings, and **those same rays bounce off it**: each is one path, O → wall → Karan, so the bounce is the ray turning, not a new beam. They run back across the ring in slow motion (~1.4 s) while the glass gives way and the squad falls, converge on Karan and knock him down. The more overcharge, the more rays come back. Before, two separate rods appeared at the wall after a pause | `sector.js` (`Ray`, `volley`, `bounce`, `fire`) |

- **After every wrong shot** the camera pulls back over the whole ring for the lesson: the shot against the sector (the dashed gold target around a narrower shot, or the red excess pulsing) with the numbers line ("Your beam covered 50° — the arc is 90°") beside the arc. During the push-in that line used to collide with the r label.
- **Camera:** after a wrong answer the 54° break view keeps Karan in the foreground (what comes next is aimed at him); too small cuts to the whole ring as the horde crosses, then pushes in on the pile-on.
- **Sounds:** `ray.volley` (many rays at once), `ray.ricochet` (the overcharge striking the wall), `swarm.surge` (the horde pouring in), `swarm.blow` (each blow in the pile-on).
- How to play: card 2 says arenas 1–3 keep π and from arena 4 π = 22/7; card 3 says "type the number in front of π, like 12 for 12π".

- **After a wrong shot**: the ghost comparison fades, the barrier forms again, a fresh squad charges it, the camera returns to the sector and the formula works one step further. Only the exact area clears an arena (open question 1).
- **TOO SMALL / TOO WIDE** now hang just below Karan (in the empty half of the ring, or below his landing spot in the push-in), never over him.
- **The Warden** (ring 7) arrives with the squad, behind the barrier, and falls only to the exact sector (any wrong shot leaves it standing).
- **How to play, card 1** is now *Watch the gap*: an animated arena with the gap, the glowing barrier, five guardians pounding on it, Karan at his spot, and O drawing the radius and sweeping out the sector. Card 3's captions: too small "The barrier breaks, but some guardians get through" · just right "The barrier and every guardian destroyed" · too big "The extra charge bounces back and knocks Karan down".
- **Sounds**: barrier forming, a guardian's blow (thud + glassy ring), the glass cracking (a snap, then crackling that speeds up until it gives way), the shatter, the pieces settling on the stone, Karan hitting the floor, the squad's footsteps as it charges in; the cannon, hatch and stasis cues are gone.
- **New art (delivered the same day, from the user's GPT strips):** the guardians' **pound** row (fists up → slam → recoil; the blow, the barrier ripple and the thud land on its impact frame), appended to the guardian sheet with `tools/add_sheet_row.py` (one scale for all frames, feet on the sheet's ground line); the sheet's cells grew to 256 × 296 px for the raised fists and its world height with them, so the robots keep their size. Karan's **cast** (seen from behind: steps in, swings his arm up, pushes his palm out toward the gap with a cyan glow, held while the charge sweeps) is the third row of the user's own hero_back sheet (idle · run · cast, drawn in the hero_back style so Karan keeps his age and height: the first GPT strip drew him older and taller and was rejected). That sheet replaced the old two-row one as delivered, after clearing small patches of grey checkerboard left between the arms; the versions rebuilt from the user's GIF (`hero_cast_animation.gif`) were not needed. The GPT specs, source strips and rejected builds are archived outside the project.
- Phases (`__se.phase`): inside → arrive → observe → tactical → scan → input → firing → result → exit.
- **The barrier break, reworked on Senior GameDev feedback** (same day): the shards hung frozen in the air and were still there when Karan walked through the gap (a bug stopped their clock), and they looked like flat cyan triangles flung outward. Now the break has weight and an ending: the glass **cracks** first (slow motion, crack lines running out from the struck part, a glass-crack sound, the camera dipping to 54° so the glass is seen face-on), then **gives way** along those same cracks: each piece falls under gravity, tumbling and glinting, lands, lies flat for a moment and shrinks away. Everything is gone about 2 s after the break, and the camera is back on the top view for the result. Sounds: crack → shatter → the pieces settling on the stone. Same break for all three outcomes (the barrier breaks in each, LXD).
- **Everything loads before the first screen** (user request, same day): one loading screen with the logo and a byte-accurate bar (the art streams in smallest file first, five at a time; the bar follows the bytes and never moves back), then the fonts, every texture uploaded to the GPU and every shader compiled. The title buttons pop in only at 100 %, and nothing can start the game before that. A file that fails or stalls never blocks: it is retried once, then drawn as its procedural stand-in. Nothing pops in or stalls once play starts (measured: no new shaders, textures or downloads during play).
- **Fewer buttons** (user request, same day): the map's star counter (★ x / 21), the speaker buttons (in-game HUD and map top bar), the HUD pause button and the final screen's "★ x / 21 · lifetime" line are gone. The HUD is the shields only (the arena card went the same day). Sound: M, or Settings on the map (and the pause menu); pause: Esc. Per-arena stars stay on the results card and the map tags.

## 0a. Top view, LXD request 2026-10-23 (earlier the same day) — draw the sector, then answer it
*"When we switch to the top view the cannon should be minimal: a point at the centre highlights and appears, a radius appears from it and sweeps to become the sector; then we zoom into the sector and the learner answers; when the answer is given we zoom out and show the whole circle."* Also: from above the cannon looked like a circle inside the arena circle, which is pedagogically wrong. - **No circle inside the circle.** Nothing round sits at the centre in the top view: no cannon or drum (retired in §0), a soft glow around O instead of an outline ring, and the floor art's carved medallion repainted as wedge flagstones converging on the centre, so even the floor points at O.
- **π = 22/7 beside the formula** (user request, same day): the dock reads *A = θ/360 × π × r²* | *π = 22/7*, the value a quieter "given" behind a hairline. On the first miss it glows with π and dissolves as 22/7 takes π's place in the worked line; from then on the formula itself carries the value, and the longer worked line keeps the full width of the dock (on the 300 px desktop dock, keeping the note there would shrink the worked line to 12–16 px).
- **Ring 7**: both camera fits also hold the whole Warden (it stands 5 m beyond the arc and towers 6.5 m), so the screen edge never cuts it; on desktop this makes ring 7's sector zoom about a third smaller than the other rings' (on phones the sector still fills the width).
- Code: `Sector.scan()` in `sector.js`; the sequence and the camera fits (`tacticalPose` = the whole ring, `sectorPose` = the sector, `heroPose` = the push-in on Karan) in `arenaScene.js`.

## 0b. LXD review 2026-10-22 — what changed
Implemented from the LXD's feedback PDF (*sector arena feedbacks.pdf*, 11 pages, every item; archived outside the project):
- **Title**: the painted evening sky, the temple behind the gate and the sun low on the right; camera low on the causeway, close to the gate; Karan stands with his back to us facing the gate (idle); carved-stone tablet buttons (glow on hover, press-down, the primary one pulses); (Continue was removed on 2026-10-27: Play always starts from the very start); How to play is a real secondary tablet; tagline removed; the dark gradient stays behind the logo column; slow camera drift toward the gate, birds, fireflies, lake mist, torch light flickering on the stone; the old box posts are free-standing torches in stone sockets.

## 1. Learning design

**Hero.** Karan, an 11–12-year-old explorer (sprite sheets `hero_front` / `hero_back`).

**Target learner.** Grades 9–10 (CBSE/NCERT *Areas Related to Circles* convention π = 22/7 so every answer is a whole number). Prerequisites: area of a circle, squaring, fractions of 360°.

**Learning objective.** Given a radius and a central angle, compute the exact sector area and understand *why* "a fraction of the circle" is the right mental model: the breach is θ/360 of the wall, so the blast must be θ/360 of the circle's area.

**Why the mechanic teaches (not just tests).**
- The radius and angle are **read off the battlefield**, not given in a text box: the point O, the radius and its sweep along the arc are drawn in world space, and the gap the guardians are trying to break through *is* the sector's arc (the barrier spans exactly θ). The abstraction (sector) is made concrete (the thing standing between Karan and the guardians).
- The answer **is** the game action. Area → laser coverage. The learner sees the consequence of the number geometrically:
  - **Exact** → the charge covers precisely the sector; the barrier cracks, gives way and falls to the floor, and every guardian behind it is destroyed; the gap is open.
  - **Too small** → the charge is a *narrower* sector (same radius, angle = θ × entered/exact). It still breaks the barrier, but the guardians it does not cover survive, walk in through the gap and **attack Karan** (−1 shield).
  - **Too large** → the charge is a *wider* sector. Every guardian falls, but the excess (red) hits the wall beside the gap and **reflects back at Karan**, knocking him down (−1 shield).
  - **Thinking time is protected.** The guardians cannot get through the barrier until the charge breaks it, so there is no clock during the computation (they keep pounding, one blow every few seconds, for urgency without pressure). Time spent in the input phase still feeds the time bonus (§4). (Before 2026-10-21 a "too slow" outcome existed; see open question 5.)
- Failure never changes the problem. The learner retries the *same* r and θ with one more level of scaffolding, so each wrong attempt converts into guided practice rather than a new random question.

**Why area maps to the angle (fixed radius).** The breach spans the wall, so the natural "shape" of the blast is the sector of the arena. Keeping r fixed and varying the angle makes the error *visible as angular coverage* — the undercharged beam visibly leaves bots standing at the edges, the overcharged beam visibly spills onto the wall — exactly as panels 10 and 11 of the wireframe show. (Mapping error to radius would hide the mistake behind the enemies.)

**Answer format (LXD 2026-10-22; π arenas 2026-10-27).** Arenas 1–3: the π is printed beside the field and the learner enters the whole number in front of it (9 for 9π). Arenas 4–7: decimals and fractions are accepted and compared at 3 decimal places: 38.5, 38.500 and 77/2 are all correct for ring 1, 38 is not. Every answer in the game is exact with π = 22/7, so no wider tolerance is needed (π = 3.14 answers are deliberately not accepted). The keypad has digits, a fraction bar and a decimal point; a physical keyboard can still type `…π` forms, which are compared on the coefficient of π.

### Misconception diagnostics
Wrong answers are matched against the most common procedural errors before a generic message is shown. (Research on fraction games found that feedback improves correctness but not misconceptions unless it is *specific to the error type* — so the game logs and names the error.)

| Code | Trigger (entered ≈ …) | Message shown |
|---|---|---|
| `full-circle` | πr² | "That is the area of the WHOLE circle. The breach is only part of it — multiply πr² by θ/360." |
| `arc-length` | (θ/360)·2πr | "That is the ARC LENGTH, not the area. Area needs r²…" |
| `not-squared` | (θ/360)·πr | "The radius was not squared. r² = r × r." |
| `circumference` | 2πr | "That is the circumference…" |
| `used-diameter` | (θ/360)·π(2r)² | "Looks like the diameter was used instead of the radius." |
| `angle`, `radius`, `r-squared` | θ, r, r² | "You entered the angle / just the radius / r² is only one step…" |
| `decimal` | 10× or ÷10 | "Check your place value." |
| `fraction-flipped` | (360/θ)·πr² | "The fraction is upside down. Use θ/360." |
| `double`, `half` | 2× / ½× | "Exactly double / half…" |
| `forgot-pi` | (θ/360)·r² | "π is missing. You have (θ/360) × r² — multiply by π." |
| `pi-as-3` | (θ/360)·3r² | "π was taken as 3. Use 22/7." |
| `under` / `over` | anything else | "About N % too small / large. Re-check each step…" |

Every feedback card also states the geometric consequence: *"You entered 100 m² — the laser covered 58° of the 90° breach."*

### Chain geometry (how the overlap is computed)
- **Lens (the passage).** Ring i's wall is open over θ_i. The passage into ring i+1 is the lens of the two circles, with its angle capped at **64°** (θ ≤ 72° uses θ itself). For equal radii that gives a lens depth of ~15 % of a diameter (12–17 % across the chain), so rings touch without merging. Half-chord c = R·sin(φ/2); the next centre sits h + √(R′² − c²) along the exit direction.
- **Breach bay.** When θ is wider than the lens (arenas 1–4 and 7), an annular-sector bay (R → R + D, D = 3.6–5.5 m) continues the open arc outward. Its two side walls are the sector's radial lines extended, so the breach reads as part of the sector.
- **Walls, pillars, seals from one boolean.** Every wall piece is "ring circle minus explicit openings minus every other disk minus every bay" (sampled at 0.5° and bisection-refined), bay walls are "bay outline minus the next disk". A carved column stands only where a wall ends at an opening: the gateposts of each passage, and where a bay wall runs into the next ring. Where a ring wall turns into its bay's side wall, and at the bay's outer corners, the walls simply meet (the side wall runs through the corner), so columns never line up one behind another. The whole chain has 16 columns, none within 4.5 m of another; they are slim (0.64 m shaft) and only a little taller than the wall. The seal is the part of ring i+1's own circle that opens toward ring i: it rises out of the floor after Karan passes, so every ring becomes a complete circle again behind him (the seventh seal closes the temple court).
- `validateChain()` checks clean answers, lens depth ≤ 20 % of the smaller diameter, single-piece seals, guardian room at every arc edge, and ≥ 1.5 m between non-neighbouring rings.

### Guardians
(LXD 2026-10-23.) The guardians are **never inside a ring before Karan**. When he arrives, the squad (one per ~1.6 m of arc, 6–30, size and tone variety) charges in from up to 7.5 m beyond the gap, through the bay or the next ring, fading in as it comes; the barrier forms across the gap and they slam into it. They stand in **one rank 1 m behind the barrier**, spaced evenly across θ, clear of the gateposts, so from above they trace the sector's arc from outside. **Pounding:** a crouch, a 0.55 m lunge into the barrier (a ripple and a thud where the blow lands), a step back; while Karan arrives and walks to his spot every guardian pounds every 0.5–1.6 s, from the top view on one random guardian every ~2–3 s. The blow is the sheet's pound row (impact on its third frame, a 0.3 m lean into the barrier); without that art the lunge's first frames stand in. **Charge:** the guardians behind the covered part of the arc fall to bolts from O; after a too-small charge the others step through the broken barrier, run at Karan and leap; O then pulses and they fall. In the top view they draw over the floor overlays, like Karan.

## 10. Tech summary
World: one Three.js scene holds the causeway, all seven rings, the temple court and ~2,000 jungle billboards (trees, plants, rocks) drawn in three instanced draw calls with a vertex-shader billboard, alpha-to-coverage edges and near-camera fading for the top views. Walls are segmented cylinders with openings cut per arc; floors are alpha-tested circles with polygon offset so overlapping lenses never z-fight.
Performance budget: pixel ratio capped at 1.5 and adapted at runtime (drops to 0.75 on slow GPUs, climbs back when frames are fast); at most 4 dynamic torch lights per arena; particle budget of 160 sprites; laser and FX shaders are compiled during arena load so the first shot does not hitch; no per-frame allocations in the hero controller. The floor is an alpha-tested opaque surface, so characters can never sort underneath it.

Three.js r170 (vendored), ES modules, no bundler. Billboard sprite sheets for characters (front/back sheets for the hero), segmented cylinder walls so any arc can sink into the ground (overlapping breaches supported), shader-drawn sector charge (`progress` front, bright edges, stripes), shader-drawn barrier (ripples per blow, GPU-animated shards for the shatter), HTML/CSS UI, Web Audio synthesis, Playwright playtests (`playtest/`). All art falls back to procedural canvases so the build is always playable.

Floor layers (`chain.js`, `FLOOR`; user 2026-10-27: "the connectors are flickering" where the causeway meets ring 1): flat surfaces that overlap must never share a height, and none may sit higher yet carry a polygon offset that pushes it back (or the reverse). Those two pulls trade places with the view angle, the screen's pixel size and the GPU, so the overlap flickers. Bottom to top: ground −0.02, the causeway's under-box −0.016, islet sand −0.012, islet grass and the bays −0.006, the causeway deck −0.002, then the ring floors (0.003 × index, the only layers with offsets: forward ones, which settle their lens overlaps). So ring 1's rim always covers the causeway's end, the causeway lies on the islet, and the bays never show the ground through them.
